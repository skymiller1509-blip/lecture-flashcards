# Lecture-to-Flashcards Tutor — demo video script

**Length:** 2–4 minutes (Devpost recommendation). Target 3 minutes.
**Format:** phone screen recording, landscape, captions on. Read aloud in
plain words, at a steady pace. Narrate what is happening, not the labels
on screen.

## The one sentence a judge should repeat

"Paste a lecture, get flashcards — no account, no API key, nothing leaves
your browser."

## Hook options (pick ONE, say it aloud, keep the clearest)

1. "This lecture is 500 words. In ten seconds it becomes flashcards you can actually study."
2. "Students re-read their notes and forget them. This turns the notes into a quiz."
3. "No AI key, no account, no upload — paste a lecture, get flashcards."

## Shot-by-shot script

### 0:00–0:05 — HOOK (sound off must still work)

- **On screen (large text, bottom third):** "PASTE A LECTURE. GET FLASHCARDS."
- **Show:** the app already open with the sample lecture loaded, cursor
  on the **Make flashcards** button. No logo, no greeting.
- **Say:** "Paste a lecture, get flashcards. No account, no API key,
  nothing leaves your browser."

### 0:05–0:20 — PROBLEM, one sentence, one named user

- **On screen:** a wall of lecture text (the sample lecture).
- **Say:** "Ada is a student. Her lecturer just dropped a full lecture
  into the class chat, and her exam is next week. Re-reading it three
  times will not make it stick — she needs to test herself."
- **Say:** "So this app turns the lecture into flashcards she can flip
  through and mark as known or not yet."

### 0:20–2:00 — THE ONE LIVE FLOW

1. **Paste / sample lecture.** "I paste the lecture in — or tap Try the
   sample lecture, which is a short lecture on photosynthesis."
2. **Tap Make flashcards.** "One tap. The app splits the lecture into
   sentences, scores them by the words that keep coming up, and turns
   the best ones into cards."
3. **Show the first card, flip it.** "What is photosynthesis? Flip —
   there is the answer, taken straight from the lecture."
4. **Show the three card shapes.** "Three kinds of card: a definition
   question, a number fill-in-the-blank, and a missing-word blank."
5. **Mark Known / Not yet.** "I know this — next. Not yet — that one
   comes back. At the end of the deck I get my score, and I can study
   just the ones I missed."
6. **Show the end-of-deck score.** "There is the score. The loop ends
   in a decision: study the unknowns again."

### 2:00–2:30 — HOW IT WORKS (30 seconds)

- **Say:** "Here is the honest part: there is no large language model
  here. No API, no key, no server. Everything runs in your browser and
  your text never leaves the page. The app scores sentences by how often
  their key words appear, then builds definition, number and
  fill-in-the-blank cards from the top sentences."
- **Say:** "That is the point: a study tool a student can use on any
  phone, for free, with no account."

### 2:30–3:00 — WHAT'S NEXT

- **Say:** "Next, I would add spaced repetition, so the cards you miss
  come back tomorrow instead of only today. Built for SLAY HACKS 2026 —
  paste a lecture, get flashcards."
- **On screen (end card):** demo link + repo link.

## Recording notes (phone)

- Android screen recorder: swipe down twice, tap Screen record,
  microphone ON, wait for the countdown.
- **Do Not Disturb ON first** — no notification mid-take.
- Landscape, browser zoom 100–125%, full screen, bookmarks bar hidden,
  extra tabs closed. Use the sample lecture so the demo never depends on
  typing or a lucky paste.
- Record one long take (~90 seconds of the live flow), then trim in
  CapCut: a judge cut at 60–75 seconds and a social cut at 25–35 seconds
  from the same footage.
- Add captions — the video may be watched without sound.
- Never claim the app uses an LLM or AI that understands the lecture.
  It is extractive: it picks sentences from the text you paste.

- [ ] Flip, Known / Not yet, and end-of-deck score all work on the phone
